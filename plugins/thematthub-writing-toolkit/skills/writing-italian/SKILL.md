# Writing Italian

Italian written by composing English sentences and converting them reads as translated,
even when every word is correct. The fix is procedural first (compose in Italian) and
stylistic second (the rules below).

Register covered here: **modern professional** Italian. Courteous, fluent, sober. Neither
the clipped directness of English business writing nor bureaucratic formulas
(*con la presente*, *in riferimento a quanto in oggetto*).

For job applications also read `references/candidature.md`.

## 1. Compose natively, never translate

1. **Fact sheet first.** Reduce the content to bare facts: names, roles, numbers, tools,
   dates. Fragments only, no sentences. If an English version of the text exists, mine it
   for facts and then put it away.
2. **Plan in Italian.** One line per paragraph stating its function
   (*apertura e candidatura*, *motivazione*, *esperienza pertinente*, *lacune*, *congedo*).
3. **Write each period in Italian from the facts.** Ask "come lo direbbe un professionista
   italiano in questa situazione?", not "how do I say this sentence in Italian?".
4. **Back-translation test.** If a sentence maps word for word onto an idiomatic English
   sentence, it is probably a calque. Rewrite it from the fact.
5. Run the final check (section 7).

When asked to translate an English text, the same applies: translate the content and the
intent paragraph by paragraph, not the sentences.

## 2. Syntax and flow

| English habit | Italian practice |
|---|---|
| Short declarative sentences in a row | Periods of 20-35 words built with subordination: relative (*che, cui, dove*), causal, concessive, gerund (*occupandomi di*), participle (*conseguito il dottorato, ...*). A short sentence is the exception, used for emphasis. |
| Relations between sentences left implicit | Connectives make them explicit: *inoltre, infatti, tuttavia, pertanto, in particolare, a questo proposito, per quanto riguarda, quanto a, d'altra parte, proprio per questo, infine*. Most sentences after the first in a paragraph should hook onto the previous one. |
| Every sentence opens with the subject | Vary the opening: a circumstance (*Nel corso del dottorato*), a topic (*Della posizione mi interessa soprattutto*), a subordinate (*Pur non avendo*), a postverbal subject (*A queste competenze si aggiunge...*). Never three sentences in a row starting with a first-person verb (*Ho... Sono... Guido...*). |
| Explicit "I" | Drop subject pronouns. *Io* only for contrast. |
| Possessives everywhere ("my team", "my PhD") | Fewer possessives: *il gruppo che coordino*, *il dottorato*, *nel lavoro quotidiano*. |
| Verbs over nouns | A moderately nominal style is normal: *la gestione di*, *nello sviluppo di*, *in qualità di*, *nell'ambito di*. Stop before bureaucratese (*effettuare, provvedere a, in merito a quanto sopra*). |
| Active voice only | Impersonal *si* and the passive are natural: *si tratta di*, *si aggiunge*, *è stato adottato*. |
| Bare assertion ("I bring X") | Courteous assertion: *ritengo di poter contribuire*, *credo che questa esperienza possa*, *sarei lieto di*. Conditional and subjunctive carry politeness, not weakness. Stay clear of servility (*mi permetto umilmente*). |
| Pro-verb echo ("I do that with...") | *Lo faccio* sounds flat. Restate with a proper verb: *è il lavoro che svolgo*. |
| Repeating the key word | Italian avoids close repetition: use a synonym, a pronoun or ellipsis. Technical terms are the exception and stay fixed. |
| Noun stacks ("MES data recommendation system") | Unpack with prepositions (*un sistema di raccomandazione alimentato dai dati del MES*), but avoid four *di* in a row. |
| Colon then a fragment list | Fine once. Italian prose prefers to integrate the items in the period. |

New information goes at the end of the period. Evaluative adjectives may precede the noun
(*una solida esperienza*, *un'ampia autonomia*); distinguishing ones follow it
(*un sistema complesso*, *clienti manifatturieri*).

## 3. Calques and false friends

| Calque (avoid) | Write instead |
|---|---|
| sono eccitato / entusiasta di candidarmi | ho letto con interesse l'annuncio |
| applicare per una posizione | candidarsi a / proporre la propria candidatura per |
| ho X anni di esperienza in | ho maturato X anni di esperienza in / nel campo di |
| sono responsabile per | sono responsabile di, mi occupo di, seguo |
| lavoro su un progetto | lavoro a un progetto, seguo un progetto |
| indirizzare un problema | affrontare un problema |
| fare una decisione / fare senso | prendere una decisione / avere senso |
| realizzare che | rendersi conto che |
| in termini di (overused) | quanto a, per, sul piano di |
| eventualmente = eventually | *infine, alla fine* (eventualmente = possibly) |

The full tables of calques, false friends and Italianised English verbs are in
`references/calques.md`. Open it for any text longer than a paragraph, and when revising a
text that "sounds translated".

**Collocations that sound native:** *maturare un'esperienza*, *acquisire / consolidare
competenze*, *conseguire una laurea / un risultato*, *ricoprire un ruolo*, *svolgere
un'attività*, *seguire un cliente / un progetto*, *curare la progettazione*, *coordinare
un gruppo*, *affiancare i colleghi*, *condurre un'analisi*, *redigere un documento*,
*mettere a frutto*, *mettere a disposizione*, *colmare una lacuna*, *portare a termine*,
*avviare un progetto*, *avere dimestichezza con*, *padronanza di*.

When the user corrects a phrase, add it to the table in `references/calques.md`.

## 4. Anglicisms

- Keep established technical terms and proper job titles in English: *machine learning,
  software, cloud, server, Technical Lead*. Echo the terms the reader's own text uses.
- **AI, LLM and software topics:** the field speaks English, so keep its terms
  (*open-weights, tool calling, agentic workflow, prompt, fine-tuning, provider, harness*)
  or simplify with a common technical term. Never coin an Italian paraphrase: *uno strato
  LLM indipendente dal provider* is heavier and less clear than *compatibile anche con
  modelli open-weights in locale*.
- Where an ordinary Italian word exists and is not clumsy, use it: *gruppo* or *team*
  (both fine), *riunione*, *riscontro*, *competenze*, *formazione*, *obiettivo*,
  *prestazioni*, *realizzazione* (delivery), *in prima persona / sul campo* (hands-on).
- English nouns are invariable and take no plural *-s*: *i tool, gli LLM, due server*.
  Masculine unless usage says otherwise (*la mail, la supply chain, la skill*).
- No Italianised English verbs (*matchare, deliverare, skillato*). No italics for
  anglicisms.

## 5. Typography and conventions

- **Accents:** *è, cioè, caffè* (grave); *perché, poiché, affinché, né, sé* (acute).
  Capital *È*, never *E'*. *Qual è* without apostrophe; *un po'* with it.
- **Capitals:** sentence case in headings and subject lines, never Title Case. Months,
  days, languages and nationalities are lowercase (*4 ottobre 2026*, *inglese*). Italian
  job titles are lowercase (*responsabile tecnico*). No capital after a colon.
- **Courtesy capitals:** *Lei, La, Le, Suo* capitalised when addressing a person in a
  formal letter. *voi, vostro* for a company stay lowercase in the modern register.
- **Dates and numbers:** *4 ottobre 2026* (*1º* only for the first); decimal comma and
  thousands point (*3,5*; *45.000 euro*); currency after the figure.
- **Commas:** none before *e* closing a list; none between subject and verb. After the
  salutation comes a comma, and the body starts lowercase on the next line.
- **Quotation marks:** «caporali» or "alte", consistently. No English-style em-dash
  asides: use commas or parentheses.
- **Euphonic d:** only before the same vowel (*ed è, ad avere*) and in *ad esempio*.
- **Abbreviations:** *Dott., Dott.ssa, Ing., Prof., Sig., Sig.ra, S.r.l., S.p.A.*
- **Lists:** items lowercase closed by semicolons with a final period, or full sentences
  each with its period. Do not mix.

## 6. Tone

Sober. Italian readers distrust superlatives and self-praise: let facts carry the claim
and introduce judgments with *ritengo*, *credo*. Restrained enthusiasm, no exclamation
marks, no slogans. Address a person with *Lei*; never *tu* unless the reader used it first.

## 7. Final check

Read the text as an Italian reader would, aloud if possible, then verify:

- [ ] No sentence fails the back-translation test; nothing from the calque tables.
- [ ] Each paragraph has at least one connective and one subordinate clause; no run of
      three short main clauses.
- [ ] Sentence openings vary; no serial first-person verbs; no stray *io*.
- [ ] No word repeated within two lines unless it is a technical term.
- [ ] Courtesy forms consistent: *Lei* and *voi* not mixed for the same addressee.
- [ ] Agreement with the writer's gender (*lieto / lieta*); ask if unknown.
- [ ] Accents, capitals, dates and numbers follow section 5.
- [ ] Anglicisms limited to technical terms and titles, invariable in the plural.
- [ ] Every sentence is understandable alone: subject, verb and the explicit request written
      out, no reference to material the reader does not have. In lists of technical requests
      each item repeats its verb (*Ci serve…*, *I dati di… ci servono per…*, *Vi chiediamo…*)
      rather than listing nouns. Also apply `writing-self-contained-text` (same plugin).
