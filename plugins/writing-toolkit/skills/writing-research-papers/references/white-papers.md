# White papers and technical or industry reports

## Audience and job
- White paper: decision makers (managers, funders, policy or procurement staff) who will not read a method
  section. Job: explain a problem, argue a solution or position, support it with evidence, recommend action.
- Technical or industry report: a project owner or client. Job: record scope, method, findings and limits so
  that someone else can check or continue the work.

## Structure
White paper: title and one-line promise; executive summary (half a page: problem, finding, recommendation,
cost or benefit with numbers); the problem and why now; the approach or position; evidence (data, case,
pilot, comparison); options and trade-offs; recommendation and next steps; about the author; references.
Technical report: executive summary first; scope and questions; data and method; findings; limitations;
recommendations; appendices (data, code, detail). Put the answer first in both, detail behind it.

## Rules
- Every claim backed by evidence that exists: a run, a dataset, a cited source, a named pilot. Quote the
  range and the conditions, not the best case.
- No marketing overclaim: no "revolutionary", no unqualified "up to", no benefit without a baseline.
  State what the approach does not do and when it does not apply.
- Compare alternatives fairly, including doing nothing and competitors, with sources dated.
- Numbers generated from runs or records (same claims inventory as a research paper).
- Hamburger paragraphs; short sentences; define terms once; figures that carry one message each.
- Say who funded or commissioned it and any interest the author or company has in the conclusion.

## Confidentiality and approval
- Client data, names, prices and internal process details stay out unless written permission exists.
- Never name a client, partner or confidential source without permission; use "a European port operator"
  only if that does not identify them. Check the contract for publication clauses.
- Company approval and IP review (legal, communications, the data owner) before anything leaves the
  organisation, including drafts shared for comment. Record who approved what and when.
- Anonymise or synthesise data; confirm that aggregates cannot be reverse-identified.
- Do not name an employer's or colleague's repository or system as the origin of the work unless the
  author directs it.
- Sending or publishing is the author's act, after approvals.

## Before release
Executive summary stands alone; every number traced; approvals recorded; claims reviewed against the
evidence by someone who did not write them; contact and version date on the cover; licence or
confidentiality marking set (public, client-confidential, internal).
